Research interests

I want to develop autonomous manipulation policies that generalize across robots and surgical instruments. I am particularly interested in combining model-based control and imitation learning for grasping, suction, cautery, and packing, alongside designing robots and end-effectors that make these skills possible.

Surgical autonomy · Oct. 2025–present

Supervised autonomous bleeding control

I developed a vision-guided dVRK system for bimanual suction and cautery in splenic trauma. My contributions include tool tracking and visual servoing, an EfficientNet tool–tissue contact detector, a custom suction adaptor, and an ex vivo perfusion platform. Collaborator-developed wound segmentation is integrated into the control loop.

0.738
Mean contact F1 in five-fold validation with entire recordings held out.
82.9%
Mean surface cauterization coverage across seven ex vivo Grade I–II injuries.

First-author manuscript submitted to ICRA 2027 · Under review

Research details

The system uses a single camera without depth estimation. I annotated approximately 100,000 training frames and extended the dVRK pipeline to record energy-tool pedal events. The experimental platform includes pulsatile perfusion, pressure monitoring, and suction loops.

Medical robot development

Robotic vertebroplasty

I designed, manufactured, and tested a UR-mounted cannula-insertion end-effector for semi-autonomous vertebroplasty. A quasi-radiolucent modular structure and synchronized drivetrain geometry reduce obstruction during fluoroscopic imaging.

The project combines mechanical packaging, structural analysis, manufacturing, and bench testing.
